Squid Debugger V1 · Power Path

Rev-1 power architecture for the SDSL Squid Debugger. Every rail is selected by TI TPS2121 priority muxes in the datasheet §10.4 XREF configuration: Stack first, then bench PSU, then USB. USB only backs up 3.3 V and 5 V. This sheet carries the block diagram, the values for every mux, the derivations behind them, and the fixes needed in the V0 capture.

01Block diagram

This replaces the whiteboard sketch. Stage A picks PSU over USB. Stage B then gives the flight stack priority over whatever stage A chose. The _PU nets are the intermediate "PSU-or-USB" nodes. The stack goes through only one mux, and every PR1 pin watches a real source rather than another mux's output. A PNG of this diagram is attached in chat so you can place it on the schematic (Place » Drawing Tools » Graphic).

Per the TPS2121 truth table, if only one input is valid (above UVLO and below OV) that input powers OUT regardless of PR1/CP2. A sagging Stack with nothing else connected keeps powering the DUT instead of dropping out. The TPS2121 also blocks reverse current, so the stack can never back-feed your bench supply or the PC's USB port.

Common to all six

  • CP2 → XREF_2V5. One shared reference; each CP2 pin leaks only ±0.1 µA.
  • ILM → 100 kΩ to GND, which gives ≈1.24 A typ (≈0.8–1.65 A). 100 kΩ is the maximum allowed RILM, so this is the lowest limit the part can set. It's short-circuit protection, not a precise 1 A connector fuse.
  • SS → 100 nF to GND, which gives ≈800 V/s soft-start. 3.3 V ramps in ≈4 ms, and a 100 µF DUT load draws ≈80 mA of inrush.
  • ST → 10 kΩ pull-up to +3V3_AUX (datasheet asks for 6–20 kΩ), plus 10 kΩ series to a spare Pico GPIO. GP14–GP19 look free on V0; check before you assign them.
  • 0.1 µF X7R on IN1, IN2 and OUT, right at the pins. Bulk COUT as in the table. Stage-A bulk doubles as the stage-B IN2 capacitor.
  • Both stages in a chain use the same switch-over voltage. Stage B's PR1 senses the stack directly, so a PSU→USB hand-off in stage A can't trip it, and no PR1 filter capacitor is needed.

Pinout check (RUX): IN1 7 · IN2 2 · OUT 1, 8 · CP2 3 · OV2 4 · OV1 5 · PR1 6 · ST 9 · ILM 10 · SS 11 · GND 12. Your existing symbol already matches this.

04Reference and USB support circuits

XREF and +3V3_AUX

In TI's §10.4 the 2.5 V on CP2 comes from an external REF3325 that is always on. The debugger has no always-on rail, so we build one from whatever sources are present.

  1. VAUX: 5 × BAT54C (common cathode). The 9 anodes go to the STK/PSU rails (8) and VBUS. The 10th anode is unused.
  2. +3V3_AUX: TLV70433DBV. 24 V max in, 3.4 µA IQ, 1 µF in and out. With only a 3.3 V source it drops out to ≈2.9 V, which is still enough.
  3. XREF_2V5: LM4040-2.5 (B grade, 0.2 %) shunt, fed from +3V3_AUX through 2.00 kΩ. Bias is 150 µA with AUX at 2.8 V and 400 µA at 3.3 V, above the ≈60 µA minimum.

CP2 = 2.5 V sits above VREF(max) = 1.10 V, so fast switchover (tFSW ≈ 5 µs) is enabled on every mux.

USB path

  1. Pico VBUS → U4 MIC2544 → +VBUS_PROT. Change R5 from 261 Ω to 464 Ω: R = 230 / 0.5 A = 460 Ω → 464 Ω E96. The 0.88 A you have now is over the USB 2.0 500 mA budget.
  2. +VBUS_PROT → U13 AP63203 → +3V3_USB. This is the "Reg" on the whiteboard. A buck is the right call here. A linear 5→3.3 V regulator at 0.5 A would burn (5 − 3.3) × 0.5 = 0.85 W, which is too much for SOT-23 parts.
  3. +VBUS_PROT → U7 IN2 (the 5 V USB fallback), with OV2 at 5.75 V against bad chargers.
  4. Budget: the Pico (~50 mA), the buck input and the 5 V output all share the 0.5 A limit.

05Derivations

Variables

VX = XREF = 2.500 V · VREF = internal OV reference, 1.06 V typ (1.01–1.10 V) · RT, RB = divider top/bottom · RH = hysteresis resistor PR1→ST · RPU = ST pull-up (10 kΩ to 3.3 V) · VST,H = 3.3 V.

1 · PR1 switch point (XREF mode)

IN1 is preferred while VPR1 > VCP2. Without hysteresis:

V_SW = V_X · (1 + R_T / R_B) → R_T = R_B · (V_SW / V_X − 1) 3.3 V stage A: R_T = 10k · (3.00/2.5 − 1) = 2.00 kΩ

2 · Adding hysteresis through ST (TI's 1 MΩ "Hyst" resistor)

KCL at PR1 with RH tied to ST. ST is high while IN1 is active and low once IN2 takes over, so the feedback is positive:

(V_IN − V_X)/R_T + (V_ST − V_X)/R_H' = V_X/R_B V_IN = V_X + R_T·[ V_X/R_B + (V_X − V_ST)/R_H' ] Falling (IN1 active, ST high, R_H' = R_H + R_PU): V_fall = V_X + R_T·[ V_X/R_B − (3.3 − V_X)/(R_H + R_PU) ] Rising (IN2 active, ST low, R_H' = R_H): V_rise = V_X + R_T·[ V_X/R_B + V_X/R_H ] U3: R_T = 2.10k, R_B = 10.0k, R_H = 54.9k V_X/R_B = 250.0 µA ; 0.8 V / 64.9k = 12.33 µA ; 2.5 V / 54.9k = 45.54 µA V_fall = 2.5 + 2.10k·(250.0 − 12.33) µA = 2.5 + 0.499 = 3.00 V V_rise = 2.5 + 2.10k·(250.0 + 45.54) µA = 2.5 + 0.621 = 3.12 V → 122 mV hysteresis

Why not copy TI's 1 MΩ: with your low-impedance dividers (RT∥RB ≈ 1.7 kΩ), 1 MΩ shifts PR1 by only ~1 mV, so it does nothing. RH has to scale with RT. Tolerance: comparator offset (≤40 mV) plus XREF (0.2 %) moves the switch point about ±1.8 %. For U3 that's 2.95–3.05 V.

3 · Overvoltage cut-off

V_OV = V_REF · (1 + R_T/R_B) R_T = R_B · (V_OV/1.06 − 1) 3.3 V: 10k·(3.8/1.06 − 1) = 25.8k → 26.1k → 3.83 V typ (3.65–3.97 V) > 3.465 V (3.3 + 5 %) ✓ 5 V: 10k·(5.75/1.06 − 1) = 44.2k → 5.75 V typ (5.47–5.96 V) > 5.25 V ✓ 12 V: 10k·(13.5/1.06 − 1) = 117k → 118k → 13.6 V typ (12.9–14.1 V) > 12.6 V ✓, < 24 V abs max ✓

This is what catches a bench PSU set to 12 V and plugged into the 5 V jack. The channel opens before the DUT sees it.

4 · Pin-voltage checks (PR1, OV ≤ 5.5 V recommended)

PR1 @ +5 %: 3.465·10/12.1 = 2.86 V 5.25·10/18.25 = 2.88 V 12.6·10/44.8 = 2.81 V ✓ OV @ trip: ≈ 1.06 V by design ✓

5 · Fast-switchover droop

V_OUT,min = V_SW − t_FSW · I_OUT / C_OUT U5 (3.3 V, 2×22 µF, 1 A): 3.00 − 5 µs·1 A/44 µF = 3.00 − 0.114 = 2.89 V U5 (3.3 V, 2×22 µF, 0.3 A): 3.00 − 0.034 = 2.97 V

This is why the stage-B outputs get 2 × 22 µF. Derate the 10 V X7R parts for DC bias at 5 V; if your DUTs draw near 1 A, go to 3 × 22 µF on +5V_OUT.

6 · Current limit

I_LIM ≈ 65.2 / R_ILM^0.861 (R in kΩ, fit to the TPS2121 table) R_ILM = 100k → 65.2 / 52.7 = 1.24 A typ (80k → 1.5 A typ, 1.0–2.0 A per datasheet)

06Fixes to the V0 capture

Must

U3 is wired from the wrong example. The 10.2 kΩ / 5 kΩ CP2 divider is TI's §10.5 XCOMP circuit, where CP2 tracks IN2. In XREF mode CP2 goes to the fixed 2.5 V reference, and every value is redone in section 03.

Must

ILM, SS and ST look unconnected on both TPS2121 instances. ILM needs 18–100 kΩ, otherwise there's no defined current limit. SS needs a capacitor.

Must

There is no Stack input. J1 pins 1–4 are the muxed outputs to the DUT, so stack rails need their own connector (assumed: J_STK, a second BM12B-GHS-TBT with the same pinout).

Should

TPS2121 parts are split across both sheets. One U3 sits on sheet 1, a second on sheet 2, and there are unannotated R? parts. Move all six muxes to Power_Mux.SchDoc, then run Tools » Annotation » Annotate Schematics.

Should

Power the CAN chips from the Pico's 3V3 (+3V3_RPI), not the muxed rail. Otherwise a stack-powered board with no USB back-powers the unpowered RP2040 through its SPI pins. Adding 33 Ω series resistors on SWDIO/SWCLK limits the reverse case into an unpowered DUT.

Should

The P1 5V↔5V_RF jumper now shorts two mux outputs when both rails are sourced. Keep it only for USB-only bench use, with a silkscreen warning "REMOVE IF 5V_RF SOURCED", or drop it.

Should

PSU banana inputs need TVS diodes (SMBJ3.3A, SMBJ5.0A ×2, SMBJ13A). Hot-plugging leads into ceramics rings to about 2× VIN, and the TVS also clamps a reversed lead to about −1 V. The TPS2121 is only rated to −0.3 V.

Tidy

Rename nets: +5V_BNN/+3.3V_BNN → +5V_PSU/+3V3_PSU, +5V_PRI → +5V_PU / +5V_OUT as appropriate, BOM typo +5V_INN. Mark BOM line 9 (TPS2113 402 Ω ILIM) and line 15 as removed.

Tidy

Set Project » Project Options » Options » Net Identifier Scope to Global (Netlabels and ports global) so the ST_* net labels connect across the flat sheets.

08Working locally in Altium, uploading later

  1. Lock in the current state first. Run Save to Server or Commit once, so the workspace holds a known V1 baseline before you branch off.
  2. Make a detached copy. Project » Project Packager → tick Unlink project from the server during packaging → save the zip to a short local path (e.g. C:\SDSL\SquidDebugger\; Altium caps full paths at 260 characters). Unzip it and open the .PrjPcb from there.
  3. Confirm it's local. The Projects panel should show no version-control icons. If any remain, open Project Options » General → sync button → Unlink, then save. Rename the project to Debugging Board V2 now, so nothing collides with V1 later.
  4. Turn on local safety nets. Preferences » Data Management » Backup: Auto Save every 10 min, keep 10 versions. Also enable Local History and tick Add autosaved documents to history. That gives you timestamped zips without git.
  5. Parts: components placed from the SDSL workspace library keep their workspace links. Stay signed in to place new ones; the project itself saves locally.
  6. Upload when ready. Right-click the project → Make Project Available Online, tick Version Control, and pick a folder. It goes up as a new workspace project with fresh history.
